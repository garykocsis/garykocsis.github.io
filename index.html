Uniswap v4 · ETHGlobal Online 2026

Standby

Future exact-output commitments backed by shared AMM liquidity

Standby began with a question: Can a liquidity pool commit to delivering a specific output amount in the future while still serving ordinary swaps? I designed and implemented a Uniswap v4 protocol that backs those commitments with shared pool liquidity.

The central design decision was to let ordinary pool activity proceed as long as the remaining execution capacity could still cover outstanding commitments. The hook checks that condition when admitting new commitments and when evaluating pool activity. When a commitment is exercised, the protocol enforces exact output and verifies delivery to the beneficiary.

I tested the backing invariant and commitment lifecycle with Foundry unit, fuzz, stateful invariant, and integration tests. Independent reference calculations checked the core economic math. I also ran the full commitment lifecycle on Base Sepolia.

RangeGuard

Swap-fee-funded impermanent loss coverage for liquidity providers

I built RangeGuard during the Atrium Uniswap v4 Hook Incubator to explore how a pool could fund capped impermanent loss coverage through its own trading activity. The hook directs a portion of swap fees into a coverage buffer. Eligible positions accrue coverage while in range, and claims settle when liquidity is withdrawn.

I used the Actual/365 Fixed day-count convention to make accrual calculations reproducible. Reactive Network monitors tick-range changes and supports periodic accrual checkpoints. I used Foundry unit, fuzz, and invariant tests to check coverage accounting, range boundaries, and withdrawal settlement.

How I Build

Before writing Solidity, I define what the protocol owes each participant, who can act, and which state changes are allowed. I turn those rules into invariants and design tests that exercise them across different sequences of actions.

One Standby test follows a commitment through three pool actions. A compatible swap proceeds. A swap that would leave too little capacity to cover the commitment reverts. The beneficiary can then still receive the exact committed output. This sequence checks that ordinary pool use can coexist with the commitment and that the hook protects it until exercise.
